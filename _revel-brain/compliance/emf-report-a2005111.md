# EMF test report A2005111-C01-R01 · Infrared sauna room

**Applies to:** to be confirmed (file name says "RS")  
**Markets:** AU, NZ

## Facts

- **Report number:** A2005111-C01-R01
- **Test lab:** Shenzhen Alpha Product Testing Co., Ltd.
- **Equipment tested:** Infrared sauna room (mica sheet heating elements on the tested model)
- **Standard:** EN 62233:2008, measurement methods for electromagnetic fields of household appliances and similar apparatus with regard to human exposure
- **Test date:** 25 May 2020
- **Report date:** 26 May 2020
- **What was measured:** Magnetic field emission, 10 Hz to 400 kHz, time domain evaluation (clause 5.5.2)
- **Measuring distance:** 10 cm
- **Operating condition:** Continuously, highest heat setting, AC 230 V 50 Hz
- **Limit:** 100% (reference level)
- **Result:** Pass

### Results (% of the limit)

| Sensor location | Measured | With measurement uncertainty |
|---|---|---|
| Front | 1.236% | 1.221% |
| Left | 1.189% | 1.175% |
| Right | 1.191% | 1.177% |
| Internal front | 1.199% | 1.184% |
| Internal left | 1.202% | 1.187% |
| Internal right | 1.291% | 1.275% |

## Customer-ready answer

<!-- Ben to approve the wording. Only for the products in applies_to. -->

An independent lab tested it to EN 62233, the European method for measuring the electromagnetic fields household appliances give off. At 10 cm from the cabin, inside and out, at the highest heat setting, the magnetic field measured about 1.3% of the reference limit: a pass.

## Staff notes

- Which Revel products this report covers must be confirmed; `applies_to` is empty until then. It covers one manufacturer's infrared sauna room series and was run on one sample model in May 2020.
- The report says it "shall not be reproduced in parts without written approval" of the lab. Quote the result; don't send customers extracts or cropped pages.
- EN 62233 measures low-frequency magnetic fields only. It doesn't support "zero EMF" or claims about electric fields or health effects.
- Oddity for whoever shares the full report: the "with measurement uncertainty" column is lower than the measured value, the wrong way round (e.g. 1.236% -> 1.221%). Every reading is still about 1.3% of the limit either way.
