The Challenge

12 big campaigns
won't get us there.

We need 12% growth in B2C retail. The traditional approach is to plan 12 big-bang campaigns and hope they hit. The problem?

High risk, slow learning

Big campaigns take weeks to plan, launch, and review. If one misses, we've lost a month and a big chunk of budget with no insight into why.

No compounding

Each campaign starts from scratch. We don't systematically build on what we learned last time.

One customer fits all

Big campaigns speak to everyone. Our data says we should be speaking directly to the Home Gym Builder and nobody else.

The Alternative

Systematic
experimentation.

Small, fast experiments across every channel. Each one teaches us something. The wins compound. The losses are cheap.

Think lean startup, applied to marketing.

The $1M Problem

Online Budget-Conscious Seekers cost us ~$1.04M in losses last year ($165 x 6,322 customers), backed by $1.8M in CAC to acquire them.

The catch: we can't identify a deal seeker before they buy. But we can control what products we put ad spend behind and which audiences we target.

The Lever

Home Gym Builders buy all-in-one trainers, functional trainers, and full rack systems. Deal seekers buy entry-level benches, single accessories, and whatever's on sale. Shift ad creative and spend toward the products HGBs are building around, and we naturally filter the audience.

The System

Start with what you want to learn.

Every experiment starts with a learning goal and a line in the sand. Define the baseline, commit to what success looks like, then run the test. A miss isn't a failure. It's a validated learning that the hypothesis was wrong.

01
Learn (the goal)

What question are we trying to answer? Define the learning goal, measure the baseline, and commit to what success looks like before anything runs.

02
Build

Design the smallest, fastest experiment that proves or disproves the hypothesis. One variable. Minimum viable test.

03
Measure

Track the metric you committed to against the baseline. Did we hit the success threshold? Don't move the goalposts.

04
Learn (the output)

Document the validated learning. Hit the threshold? Scale it. Missed? That's valuable too. Either way, it feeds the next hypothesis.

Experiment Card Template
We want to learn: [the question we're trying to answer]

Because [observation / data],
we believe [this change]
will cause [expected outcome].

Baseline: [current metric value, measured before we start]
Success looks like: [specific threshold we commit to before running]
Duration: [how long we run it]
Example
We want to learn: Does lifestyle imagery outperform product shots for HGB prospecting?

Because convenience is the #1 JTBD for Home Gym Builders, we believe ad creative showing "garage gym lifestyle" imagery will cause a 20% increase in CTR for Meta prospecting campaigns.

Baseline: Current Meta prospecting CTR is 1.2%
Success looks like: CTR hits 1.4%+ sustained over 2 weeks
Duration: 2 weeks

Prioritisation

Not all experiments are equal.

Score every experiment idea before you run it. Run the highest-scoring first. This prevents us from burning time on low-impact tests.

I
Impact

If this works, how much does it move revenue or leads?

C
Confidence

How sure are we, based on data (not gut feel)?

E
Ease

How fast and cheap can we ship and measure this?

ICE Score
(Impact + Confidence + Ease) / 3

Score each 1-10. Run the highest-scoring experiment first.

Example ICE Scoring
ExperimentICEScore
Homepage hero: "Build Your Home Gym" CTA 978 8.0
Email: transformation story subject lines 769 7.3
Social: garage gym UGC series 658 6.3
Blog: inline product recs in HGB content 756 6.0
Rule of thumb: If you can't score it, you don't understand it well enough to test it yet. Go gather more data first.

Cadence

The fortnightly sprint.

Every fortnight at the marketing meeting, each person shares a 5-minute update on their experiments. Same format, every time. Two weeks gives experiments enough data to be meaningful.

1
What's running?

Quick status on live experiments. Any issues?

2
What did we learn?

Share results from completed experiments. Win or loss, the learning is the value.

3
What's next?

Announce the next experiment launching. Get team input on the hypothesis.

+
Cross-pollinate

A learning in one channel often sparks an experiment in another. That's the compound effect.

Experiment Velocity Target
1
Experiment per person per fortnight
~78
Total experiments per year

3 people x 1 experiment/fortnight x 26 fortnights = ~78 experiments per year. If even 20% are winners, that's 15+ compounding improvements across every channel driving us toward 12% growth.

The rule: It's better to run a small experiment and learn something than to plan a perfect one that never launches.

Measuring Success

How we know it's working.

We track two layers: the experiments themselves (are we learning and improving?) and the business outcomes they drive (are we growing?).

Experiment Health Metrics
Experiment velocity 1 per person / fortnight

Are we running enough experiments to compound?

Win rate Target 20%+

Industry average is 10-30%. If we're below 10%, we're not being bold enough. Above 30%, not being risky enough.

Learning rate 100%

Every experiment, win or loss, must have a documented learning. No wasted tests.

Time to result 2-4 weeks

Most experiments should resolve within a month. If it takes longer, scope it smaller.
