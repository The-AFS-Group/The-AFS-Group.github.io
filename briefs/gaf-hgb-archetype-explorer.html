01  /  The person

A project, not a purchase

The Home Gym Builder is in project mode. He is not shopping for a piece of equipment, he is building a room. That single distinction changes what he needs from us at every stage.

David Fowler, 38

Our persona anchor. Fictional, assembled from survey and call data, so treat him as an archetype and never as a contact.

  • The spaceA garage or spare room. Often sloped, often tight. “The 24 inch depth was sort of the furthest we'd go.”
  • The project$4k plus, planned over weeks, not bought in a session.
  • The decisionHousehold. A partner is in the room for 15% of decisions outright, and consulted in most of the rest.
  • The triggerA renovation, a move, quitting the gym, or a sale that gives him a date.
  • He cross-shopsRogue, Rep Fitness, Force USA elsewhere, Vulcan, Nordic.
  • He worries aboutSpace and fit first. Then freight. Then stock.

How we win him: take the design burden off him. The moment somebody else holds the plan, his mood flips from anxious to excited, and the basket grows on its own.

The definition

What counts as a Home Gym Builder sale

Path 1  ·  180 builds

A single anchor, but only if it is an all-in-one trainer or home gym. Those units are a complete gym in one box. A lone treadmill, bike, rower or rack does not count on its own.

Path 2  ·  832 builds

Any anchor (all-in-one, home gym, rig, power rack, or genuine cardio) plus at least one attachment. The most common build by a wide margin.

Path 3  ·  245 builds

Two or more anchors, any attachment count. Catches the trainer plus treadmill build.

All three paths require a cart over $3,376. Locked v4 with Adam Carter, 4 Aug 2026, superseding the earlier two-path rule. Saunas, ice baths and pin or plate loaded machines count as attachments only, never as the anchor. Counted cumulatively from 1 July 2025 against the 10,000-by-2030 BHAG. The bit that matters

The obvious comparison between these two tables is wrong

$2,376 is not an improvement on $1,271. Lens A is archetype segmented and net of CAC; Lens B is cart-rule defined and gross. They are different populations on different bases. What does travel between them:

The v4 rule admits only big baskets, so the population we count for the BHAG is richer than the archetype population, and the sales assisted premium is much smaller inside it. On the cohort we count, assisted is 1.33 times self serve, not 2.5 times. The $3,376 plus anchor gate does the work the consultant used to, by excluding the cheap online carts that dragged the archetype model's online figure down to $502.

Two consequences. The consultant's value is real but is overstated by the 2.5 times number whenever it is quoted against the cohort we count. And the BHAG profit floor should not be modelled at $1,271 a build: at the cohort's actual median gross profit, 10,000 builds is roughly $24m of gross profit, before CAC and before any Phase 2 reorder. The strategy is stronger than the $12.7m floor in the operating manual, not weaker.

The honest framing for the team is "self serve is already profitable on big baskets and we want more of them", not "self serve is a poor cousin at half the profit". Which number we put in front of the team is an open decision for Josh.

05  /  The refusal

Who this is not for

The refusal is load bearing. It is not a limitation we apologise for, it is the thing that makes the economics work. The journey must never quietly widen to “for everyone”.

In scope

The Home Gym Builder

Gets the full programme: the designer, consultant time where the lead is worth it, freight solutions, and Phase 2 follow up.

  • Project mode, garage or spare room
  • Cart over $3,376 with a real anchor
  • Household decision, weeks not minutes
  • Comes back and refers
Out of scope

The budget conscious single SKU shopper

Self serve only. No consultant time, no freight subsidy, no design session. Not because we dislike them, because they lose money at a median of $165 a customer.

  • One item, price led
  • Cross-shops on shipping alone
  • Does not return, does not refer
  • Absorbs the time the builder needs
06  /  The brief this writes

What the journey tells us to build

Five priorities fall straight out of the evidence above. Each one answers a barrier this customer names in his own words.

Priority 01

Own the handoff into design

“I just don't know what I want” is a design problem, not a browsing problem. Route the overwhelmed browser into the designer, especially off the all-in-one trainer collection.

Priority 02

Ask for the space first

Sloped garages, 24 inch rack limits, ceiling height. Take the room's dimensions up front and the number one barrier disappears.

Priority 03

Tell the truth about freight and stock

Both are the top website confusion topics and the last live objections at purchase. Show them honestly before checkout, not after.

Priority 04

Design for two people

The saved design has to be shareable, because a partner is in this decision far more often than the funnel assumes.

Priority 05

Save every design

The saved design is the reorder asset and the referral asset. It is the object that makes the money phrase literally true.
