The metric

Two qualifying paths

A single strict "1 anchor + 2 attachments" test undercounts our strongest builders — a cart of an All-In-One trainer plus a treadmill is two centrepieces but reads as only one anchor and one attachment. A second path fixes that.

Path A · Anchor + build-out

One centrepiece, kitted out

One anchor product, plus at least two attachment items, with a total cart value over the threshold. The everyday home-gym build.

1 anchor  +  2 attachments  +  cart > $3,376
648 of the 894 qualifying sales (72%)
Path B · Multi-anchor

Two centrepieces in one cart

Two or more anchor products, with a total cart value over the threshold — regardless of attachment count. Two centrepieces is self-evidently a gym build.

2+ anchors  +  cart > $3,376
246 of the 894 qualifying sales (28%)

The $3,376 gate is the average Home Gym Builder cart value from our archetype economics. The lever that makes the metric decisive is the anchor list, not the gate — anchors are project-defining training centrepieces only.

Is the metric decisive?

It rejects nearly half of big carts as "not a build"

Decisiveness is the whole point — the measure has to separate a Home Gym Builder from a Performance Optimiser and a Budget-Conscious Seeker. Of the 1,696 GAF AU carts a year that clear the $3,376 value gate, the composition rule passes 894. Value alone is not enough; the build has to be there.

Method & assumptions

How the number was built

So the team can trust, challenge and reproduce it.

Scope. Sales orders on the GAF AU + GAF Online-AU departments (matching the standing NetSuite saved search 6022), dated the last 12 months, excluding cancelled and closed orders. This excludes GAF Commercial, Wholesale, Warranty, NZ and Export. ~15,400 orders. Read-only throughout.
Value gate. Order total over $3,376, inclusive of GST and freight (the cart value the customer is charged). 1,696 orders clear it.
Composition. For each cart, the priced order lines were taken (kit parents counted once, their exploded component and box lines collapsed to the parent, tax/discount/shipping lines dropped), each distinct product mapped to its sub-category, then tagged anchor / attachment / neither and the two-path rule applied.
Attachments are counted by distinct SKU — two different attachment products, per Adam's "any 2 other SKUs" rule. A single line bought in quantity 2 is one attachment, not two.
Coverage. 96% of the 14,200 priced lines matched the categorisation sheet directly; the remaining 4% (REP racks and Ares trainers, STEPR cardio, Impulse machines, flooring and plate bundles) were classified from their NetSuite descriptions. Unresolved lines were treated as "neither" (the conservative direction — it cannot manufacture a false builder).
Residual caveats, flagged not hidden. The saved search's staff/warranty/test order-source exclusion is not yet applied in this run (small effect). The bundle and variant SKUs should be folded into Chris's categorisation sheet so the KPI runs automatically. Re-running with both will firm the number.
