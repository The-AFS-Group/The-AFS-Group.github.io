02 · Who he is

Three common misconceptions

These are easy assumptions to make, and each one quietly shapes how we write, merchandise and answer the phone. All three turn out to be contradicted by our own call data.

Myth 01
He's a beginner
He already trains.
Only 14 of 964 are complete beginners. 678 are some-experience to advanced. Worth remembering before we write to him as though he is starting out.
Myth 02
He's chasing the cheapest
His budget bends.
Price is the most-mentioned friction in the whole dataset (485), yet only 19% hold a strict maximum and 79% have some flexibility. Compared to a competitor we read as similar or cheaper 363 times against premium 73. He is not looking for the cheapest option, he is looking to be convinced the number is right.
Myth 03
He rings us to buy
He rings us because he's stuck.
78% of calls are at consideration stage, not decision. He is asking to be helped, not sold to.
03 · Jobs to be done

He hires us for three jobs at once

Every call was coded for a functional, an emotional and a social job. The functional one is what he says out loud. The other two are why he actually buys. Underneath each job sits the language he uses himself, with the share of the cohort who used it.

This is the row that opens the film. 4.7% name queueing directly, which is why Gary is standing there with his arms folded.

They are not buying equipment. They are buying back time, progress and control. The three things their old gym was taking from them.

05 · Motivations & triggers

He is buying time, not equipment

Separate the trigger from the catalyst. The trigger is what puts him in the market, and it is always a change in his life: he has outgrown what he owns (73), a renovation or move has made room (54), an injury has changed what he needs (20). The catalyst is what makes him act on a particular Tuesday, and that is usually a deadline (113).

A sale is a catalyst, never a trigger. Nobody wants a home gym because it is discounted. Confusing the two sends us upstream in the wrong direction: we end up trying to buy demand in November that we should have been building in August. Caveat worth stating plainly: the coded trigger field is the weakest in the dataset, roughly two thirds of entries are circular ("wants to buy gym equipment"), so treat these as directional, not precise.

Every gym we design pays us three times: to build it, to grow it, to refer the next one.

06 · How we count him

What counts as a Home Gym Builder build

Everything above describes who he is. This is how we turn that into a number we can track. The rule was locked with Adam Carter on 4 August 2026, and it is what feeds the 10,000-by-2030 BHAG tracker.

The value gate Cart > $3,376 Every path below must also clear this.
Path 1Centrepiece

A single anchor, but only if it is an All-In-One Trainer or a Home Gym / Multi-Station unit. A lone treadmill, bike, rower or rack does not qualify on its own.

AIO or Home Gym  +  cart > $3,376
180 builds
Path 2Anchor + attachment

Any anchor product plus at least one attachment item. The everyday build, and by far the most common.

1 anchor  +  1 attachment  +  cart > $3,376
832 builds
Path 3Multi-anchor

Two or more anchors in the same cart, regardless of how many attachments come with them.

2+ anchors  +  cart > $3,376
245 builds
Path 1 is deliberately narrow, and that is what makes the metric defensible. It was tightened on 4 August from "any anchor" to "All-In-One or Home Gym only". Widen it back and a customer who buys a single treadmill and nothing else becomes a "home gym build", which would quietly turn this into a big-ticket-sales metric rather than a home-gym one.

June is 3.3× the quietest month. For this customer Black Friday is not a general traffic event, it is one of only two moments in the year when project-mode buyers actually commit. But read it correctly: these months convert demand, they do not create it. The trigger that put him in the market happened weeks or months earlier, and the sale only decided the date. The implication is uncomfortable and worth saying out loud: the pipeline has to be full before November, not during it.

One number to handle carefully. You may know this customer as “$1,271 median profit, 2.5× online”. That comes from the archetype model, which is a different population from the cart-rule cohort we now count. On the cohort we count, the assisted premium is 1.33×, not 2.5×, and median gross profit is higher, not lower. Do not quote “$1,271 per home gym build”. Which figure becomes official is an open decision.
