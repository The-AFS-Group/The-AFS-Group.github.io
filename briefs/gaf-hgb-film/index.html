01 · Who he is

Three things the team usually gets wrong

Each of these is a belief that shapes how we write, merchandise and answer the phone. Each one is contradicted by our own call data.

Myth 01
He's a beginner
He already trains.
Only 14 of 964 are complete beginners. 678 are some-experience to advanced. Do not write to him like he's starting out.
Myth 02
He's chasing the cheapest
His budget bends.
Price is the most-mentioned friction in the whole dataset (485), yet only 19% hold a strict maximum and 79% have some flexibility. Compared to a competitor we read as similar or cheaper 363 times against premium 73. He is not looking for the cheapest option, he is looking to be convinced the number is right.
Myth 03
He rings us to buy
He rings us because he's stuck.
78% of calls are at consideration stage, not decision. He is asking to be helped, not sold to.

Separate the trigger from the catalyst. The trigger is what puts him in the market, and it is always a change in his life: he has outgrown what he owns (73), a renovation or move has made room (54), an injury has changed what he needs (20). The catalyst is what makes him act on a particular Tuesday, and that is usually a deadline (113).

A sale is a catalyst, never a trigger. Nobody wants a home gym because it is discounted. Confusing the two sends us upstream in the wrong direction: we end up trying to buy demand in November that we should have been building in August. Caveat worth stating plainly — the coded trigger field is the weakest in the dataset, roughly two thirds of entries are circular ("wants to buy gym equipment"), so treat these as directional, not precise.

June is 3.3× the quietest month. For this customer Black Friday is not a general traffic event, it is one of only two moments in the year when project-mode buyers actually commit. But read it correctly: these months convert demand, they do not create it. The trigger that put him in the market happened weeks or months earlier, and the sale only decided the date. The implication is uncomfortable and worth saying out loud — the pipeline has to be full before November, not during it.

One number to handle carefully. You may know this customer as “$1,271 median profit, 2.5× online”. That comes from the archetype model, which is a different population from the cart-rule cohort we now count. On the cohort we count, the assisted premium is 1.33×, not 2.5×, and median gross profit is higher, not lower. Do not quote “$1,271 per home gym build”. Which figure becomes official is an open decision.
