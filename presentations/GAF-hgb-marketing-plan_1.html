Home Gym Builder Marketing Plan

If every Gym and Fitness customer looked like the Home Gym Builder, this would be an entirely different, far more profitable business. This plan is not about protecting a niche - it is about making the Home Gym Builder GAF's default customer across ecommerce, paid media, social, email, sales, wholesale, commercial and retail alike, while wholesale, commercial and retail keep growing in their own right too.

Front matter

Executive Summary

Home Gym Builders already outperform every other Gym and Fitness customer: 9% of customers deliver 27.3% of revenue, at a $4,031 average order value and roughly 2.5× the profit of an average sale. This plan turns that outperformance into the default expectation, not the exception - fixing the website's mobile and checkout experience, sharpening paid and social spend toward what actually converts, turning a 43,370-contact email list into the highest-return channel in the mix, and treating a wholesale, commercial or retail sale one step removed from a home gym exactly like a direct one.

Front matter

Tone of Voice & Language

Too much of our marketing currently sounds like a brand reseller: it leads with the logo on the box (Nike, Assault, REP, Force USA, Hyrox) rather than with who is actually building the room. That has to flip. GAF is Australia's home gym builder and its largest equipment supplier, not a marketplace that happens to stock good gear. The brands we carry are proof of that authority, not the headline - and the authority itself comes from us: our reviews, our service, our people and our reason for doing this at all. Every piece of copy should say who we are, not just who we sell.

The rule: identity first, capability second, brand third

1

Identity. Open on who we are and who we build for - "Australia's home gym builder" - not on a product name.

2

Capability. Say what we actually do for the customer (design, supply, deliver, install, support the whole room), before naming a single SKU.

3

Brand as proof, not pitch. Exclusive access to Nike, Assault, REP, Powerblock and our Hyrox partnership are evidence GAF can back up the claim above. They close the argument; they don't open it.

Who we are, not just who we sell

A brand name on the box explains what someone is buying. It doesn't explain why they should buy it from us - and that second story is ours to tell, in every channel, not filed away on an "About Us" page nobody reads.

We're a brand in our own right, not a reseller shopfront

GAF designs, builds and stands behind the whole room, whatever equipment sits inside it - "curated and built by GAF" carries more weight than any single logo we stock.

Our reviews do the talking

A review score earned one build at a time is proof no ad can buy - state it plainly, up front, not tucked into a footer badge.

Real people, not a chatbot

Every phone call and Experience Centre visit is answered by someone who has actually helped build a home gym before, not a script or an AI voice. "Talk to a real person who's done this before" is a genuine point of difference - say it.

The mission is the point, not the sign-off

We're here to help Australians live longer, happier, healthier lives - every build we do, big or small, is in service of that, and it's worth saying plainly rather than assuming it's understood.

Instead of leading with…Lead with…
"Shop the new Nike Strength range""Your home gym, done properly - now with Nike Strength inside it"
"REP fitness equipment now in stock""We'll design your home gym around your space. This week that includes new REP gear."
Product-first ad, brand logo as heroRoom-and-outcome-first ad, brand logo as a supporting credential
A reviews badge tucked in the footer"Read what the last home gym builder we helped had to say" up front
"Contact us" / a support ticket form"Talk to a real person who's built a home gym before"

Language habits to build

  • Talk to the customer like someone who already trains, because he almost always does - only 14 of the last 964 sales conversations we analysed were with a complete beginner. Skip the "what is a squat rack" tone.
  • Talk about the room, not the unit - "build," "space," "the rest of the room" - because that's how Home Gym Builders actually think and buy.
  • Use "we" for GAF and "you" for the customer; keep it warm, plain and Australian - an expert mate, not a salesperson and not a hype account.
  • Every exclusive brand mention should finish this sentence: "…which means GAF can build you something a generic reseller can't." If it can't, cut the brand name back to a supporting credit.
  • When trust or support comes up, say it plainly - the review score, and that a real person answers the phone - rather than filing it under a policy page.
  • Tie longer copy back to the mission - helping Australians live longer, happier, healthier lives - rather than ending on price or a generic CTA alone.

Applies to anything customer-facing - ads, social, email, web copy, in-store and wholesale materials.

01 · Strategy & Ambition

The Alignment Test

Every department in this plan can generate ideas faster than the business can resource them. Before anything gets briefed, built or booked, it has to answer at least two of these four questions clearly:

Does it reach, convert or retain a Home Gym Builder - or a wholesale, commercial or retail sale one step removed from one?

1

Does it move someone toward, or itself count as, a qualifying order (cart over $3,376, anchor + attach)?

2

Can the same piece of work be reused in at least one more channel or department without a full rebuild?

3

Does it protect or grow gross profit per customer - not just traffic, reach or revenue?

If a task can't clear that bar, don't kill it outright - pivot it first. Usually that means changing the audience it's aimed at, the channel mix it runs on, or the call to action, not the creative itself. If it still can't clear the bar after that, park it. This test is the day-to-day filter for what the team works on, replacing quarterly targets as the thing every task is measured against.

02 · Channel & Digital Build

Department: Ecommerce (Website)

Objective. Make gymandfitness.com.au the easiest place in Australia to design, price and buy a complete home gym, at the highest AOV the range supports.

Strategy. The website's biggest problem isn't attracting people - it's what happens once they arrive. Mobile carries 81% of all traffic but converts at less than half the rate of desktop, so fixes need to work on a phone first. The upside case is just as clear: assisted builds carry a median gross profit of $2,376 versus $1,782 self-serve, so every friction point removed compounds.

Where the website loses people
Sitewide funnel, last 4 weeks (~226,500 sessions)

Homepage bounce rate has held in a 64-71% band since late June. Roughly 77% of everyone who reaches checkout abandons it - worse than the ~70% average Baymard Institute reports across 50 industry studies. Their top reasons: unexpected costs (40%), slow delivery (20%), payment distrust (19%), forced account creation (18%), a checkout that's too long (17%).

Execution

  • Rebuild the homepage around Gary's language and job-to-be-done ("design your home gym").
  • Build 3–5 pre-configured Home Gym bundles (Apartment Gym, Garage Gym, Strength Studio), each priced to clear the $3,376 qualifying gate
  • Add "Complete your build" / "Customers also bought" modules on every anchor product page and in cart, seeded from real attach data
  • Add a pop-up or clear communication at checkout around delivery and install options, alongside a visible returns/warranty statement at cart and checkout
  • Add a persistent "Talk to a home gym expert" module (chat, phone, book-a-call) and an "Visit the Experience Centre" booking option on every anchor-category page and in cart
  • Audit and fix Core Web Vitals / page-load speed on mobile PDPs and category pages, starting with the highest-traffic paid-referral pages (one live example: a single collection page recently drove 23,361 sessions at 85.9% bounce and just 6 completed orders, almost all of it paid traffic) - a common silent cause of the mobile conversion gap above
  • Use Clarity to find friction and customer pain points across the site, and fix them
  • Optimise the AI Gym Designer tool and add it to the homepage
  • Build a Home Gym Builder gallery landing page where customers submit photos of their finished build, for a chance to win a $100 GAF gift card each month - a standing source of real-room social proof and UGC, feeding straight into the Organic Social content pillars and Sales's case-study library

Department: Paid Media & Search

Objective. Capture and convert Home Gym Builder intent wherever it shows up - paid search, paid social, AI answer engines - without the whole growth engine sitting on one channel.

Strategy. Our own 90-day channel data shows each paid channel already does a different job. Meanwhile the business is structurally exposed: 68% of all website traffic is paid, and the single largest overall traffic source - social referrer, at 86,692 sessions - converts at just 0.013%, effectively nothing.

AI-answer traffic (ChatGPT/Gemini-style) is tiny today (2,192 sessions) but grew 378% in 90 days and already converts best of all - the fastest-growing, most fragile-to-ignore channel in the whole mix.

Worth naming plainly: direct traffic is currently GAF's best-converting channel at 0.53%, more than double paid's 0.17% and roughly 40× social referrer's 0.013%. It's a small share of sessions today, but it's the clearest evidence yet for this plan's retention, referral and brand-building emphasis elsewhere - the customers who already trust GAF enough to type the URL in convert far better than any paid channel currently delivers.

Execution

  • Rebuild campaign structure around TOF/MOF/BOF across Google and Meta - one clear job per ad, with ongoing A/B testing of creative and CTA at every stage
  • Shift paid social's brief toward the job it does well - awareness and front-door - and let Paid Shopping and Search carry the "catch and convert" role the data shows they own
  • Start a standing blog programme targeting Gary's real search language ("home gym for a small garage," "quiet home gym equipment"), not generic product SEO
  • Add a GEO (generative engine optimisation) workstream: structured, clearly quotable product and FAQ data so GAF gets cited in ChatGPT/Gemini/AI Overviews shopping answers
  • Run competitor conquesting in paid search - bid on Rebel Sport, BLK BOX, Bodycraft, Fitness Warehouse and similar terms with creative that leads on the home-gym-builder claim, not just price
  • Run geo-targeted campaigns in the weeks after Hyrox and similar events in each host city, aimed at the "just trained, just motivated" audience
  • Test geo/behavioural targeting toward "just moved" and renovation-intent audiences - a natural trigger moment for a first home gym build that nothing currently targets
  • Protect branded search rather than ceding it while the organic programme builds - paid and organic branded search reinforce each other
  • Set up and optimise a Google Business Profile for the Experience Centre, to capture "home gym builder near me"-style local search intent the EC currently isn't chasing
  • Report share of voice, branded search volume and blog-driven sessions monthly alongside paid metrics

02 · Channel & Digital Build

Department: Email, SMS, Referral & Retention

The Stats

Referred customers carry 16% higher lifetime value and 18% lower churn than average (Wharton study, via Extole); 88% of consumers trust a recommendation from someone they know over any other channel (Nielsen); referred customers show 150% higher average order value than other customers (Forrester). Internally, the case is already proven: second builds are only 13% of repeat orders but generate 78% of repeat revenue.

Objective. Turn the list GAF already owns - 43,370 email contacts - into the best-performing channel in the plan, on nurture, retention and referral, not top-of-funnel acquisition.

Strategy. Ecommerce benchmarks give a clear bar: welcome-series emails typically open at 40–60% versus 18–25% for regular campaigns, abandoned-cart emails recover roughly 5–10% of carts, and top ecommerce brands earn 30–40% of total revenue from email and SMS combined (Klaviyo/industry benchmarks via Darkroom).

Execution

  • Grow the list itself, not just what's already on it - a gated content offer, Experience Centre visit capture, a quiz/gift-with-signup, and a stronger checkout opt-in prompt, each tracked by source
  • Rebuild the welcome series around the real Home Gym Builder buying cycle (Trigger → Research → Consultation → Build & Install → Advocate), not a generic first-order discount
  • Add SMS for time-sensitive moments only - cart/checkout abandonment nudges and sales-booking confirmations/reminders
  • Build a retention flow after a build completes: check-in, usage tips, then a second-build/expansion prompt timed around the quietest months in the build cycle
  • Launch a post-purchase referral incentive (credit or bundle discount for both referrer and referred), tracked distinctly like the affiliate programme
  • Add dynamic content blocks by browsing/purchase behaviour - show the attachments someone hasn't bought yet, not a generic newsletter
  • Turn permissioned build case studies from Sales's feedback loop into a recurring email/blog series
  • Report flow revenue share and open/conversion rate by flow monthly against the benchmark ranges above

04 · Sales, Commercial & Measurement

Department: Sales Team

Objective. Turn every sales conversation into a full home-gym build, and every build into the next piece of marketing evidence.

Strategy. Assisted selling roughly doubles the profit of a self-serve sale. Phone consultation is, on our own channel data, the single most profitable path to a sale. And because only 14 of the last 964 sales calls were with a genuine beginner, the room to win isn't explaining the basics - it's removing confidence and technical friction.

Assisted selling roughly doubles profit
Median profit per order, net of acquisition cost, by archetype

Sales-led actions

  • Use Home Gym Builder language on every call: open on training goals and the room, not budget
  • Offer a small bundle discount specifically for qualifying anchor + attachment combinations
  • Ask two extra discovery questions on every call - training goal, and what "done" looks like for the room - logged as structured CRM fields
  • After every completed build, request a review, a photo/video, and permission to use it as a case study
  • Run an ongoing feedback survey on what almost stopped recent customers buying, to keep barrier data current
  • Where a customer declines to build now, capture the specific blocker so marketing can re-target with the right message later

Marketing's lead-gen support for Sales

Risks & Dependencies

  • Wholesale and Retail targets are still TBC. The Ecommerce (D2C) build target is confirmed at 2,000 qualifying builds a year and gross profit growth is confirmed at 16.9% year on year; Wholesale and Retail can't be reported against a baseline until the Board confirms the wider BHAG - though execution (Ecommerce fixes, Paid Media tests) can start immediately regardless.
  • Mobile and checkout fixes carry the most weight. A large share of the plan's expected upside depends on the Ecommerce execution list landing well; if it slips, the paid, social and email work built on top of it has less to convert against.
  • Paid remains a single point of exposure. 68% of all traffic is paid - the "more good, less bad" reallocation reduces this over time, but doesn't remove the dependency this year.
  • Sales team capacity. Assisted selling drives most of the profit in this plan; growing the volume of consultations and EC visits assumes the sales team can absorb that load without service quality slipping.
  • Wholesale and retail partner cooperation isn't guaranteed. The reporting and content-sharing asked of Wholesale and Retail partners here depends on their willingness to participate, not just GAF's own execution.
  • The Experience Centre is a new, largely untested channel. Several tasks (local SEO, sales lead-gen, booking flows) assume it performs as a discovery and consultation channel; that's a reasonable bet, not yet a proven one.
04 · Sales, Commercial & Measurement

Why This Works: The Evidence

  • The Home Gym Builder is already GAF's best customer by every profitability measure: 2.5× the profit of an average sale, at a $4,031 AOV.
  • It's the only archetype that survives with zero human help. Sold online with no consultant, other archetypes lose money on average - the Home Gym Builder makes $502 in profit net of acquisition cost.
  • Assisted selling roughly doubles it again: $1,271 median profit net of CAC assisted versus $502 online.
  • Repeat builds are rare but disproportionately valuable: 13% of repeat orders are second builds, generating 78% of repeat revenue.
  • The business is more exposed to a single channel than it looks. 68% of all traffic is paid, and the single largest traffic source converts at just 0.013%.
  • Referral and retention economics proven elsewhere point the same direction as GAF's own numbers: 16% higher lifetime value, 18% lower churn, 88% trust a personal recommendation over any ad.
  • Checkout is GAF's single biggest recoverable leak. Roughly 77% of checkout starters abandon - worse than the ~70% Baymard Institute average.

No target in this plan is locked while the BHAG is under review - but the direction isn't in question. Every stat above points the same way: more Home Gym Builders, converted with less friction, kept longer, and referred more often.

Bringing It Together

Every part of this plan comes back to one person: Gary. He already trains, his budget bends more than he lets on, and his build follows a pattern the business now understands in detail. The job across every department is the same - make it easier for him to find GAF, easier to trust the room he's about to build, and easier to come back for the next one.

The four-part system, in one line each

  1. Strategy & Ambition sets the target and the funnel every other part plugs into.
  2. Channel & Digital Build fixes the website, sharpens paid and social, and turns email into the highest-return channel in the plan.
  3. Field, Calendar & Creative keeps every department working from the same content, calendar and creator relationships, instead of nine separate ones.
  4. Sales, Commercial & Measurement turns the sales floor, wholesale and retail into three more paths to the same qualifying build, and holds the whole plan to the evidence above.

None of this needs the BHAG locked to start. The Alignment Test above is already the day-to-day filter: does it reach or convert a Home Gym Builder, does it move someone toward a qualifying order, can it be reused, does it protect gross profit. Anything that clears two of those four is worth doing now, whatever number the Board eventually confirms.

What "working" looks like a year from now: a homepage, checkout and email programme built around Gary's language instead of a product catalogue; a paid and social mix that spends where the data says to, not where it always has; a wholesale and retail motion that counts the same way a consumer build does; and a business where more of GAF's customers look like its best one.
