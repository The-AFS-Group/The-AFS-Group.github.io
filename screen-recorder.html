Install & use

What this is

A Chrome extension for the AFS team that records your screen, webcam, and microphone into one video. A single draggable webcam head sits on the page - drag it wherever you like and that is exactly where it appears in the recording. A slim recorder toolbar, plus the extension icon itself, gives you pause and stop at any time. Recordings are saved locally to your machine. To share, drag the file into Google Drive and copy the link.

FeatureDetail
CameraA live webcam head you drag onto the page; filmed in place. Pick which camera in the popup.
AudioMicrophone captured with the screen. Pick which mic in the popup.
ControlsPause and stop from the buttons on your webcam head (faint until you hover, then solid), the slim toolbar, or the extension icon. A 3-2-1 countdown before recording.

Output.webm file saved to your Downloads folder
SharingDrag the file into Google Drive for a shareable link
CostZero. No servers, no accounts, no subscriptions

Install on Chrome / Chromebook

  1. Download the zip above and unzip it. You should get a folder with manifest.json, a src/ folder, and an icons/ folder directly inside (no nested subfolder). On ChromeOS, open Files, find the zip, right-click and choose Extract.
  2. Move the unzipped folder somewhere permanent before loading it into Chrome. Chrome reads the extension files directly from the folder you point it at - it does not copy them. If that folder gets deleted or cleaned up, the extension breaks.

    Where to put it:
    ChromeOS / Chromebook: Open Files, go to My Files, and create a folder called Extensions. Move the unzipped folder there so the path is My Files › Extensions › AFS Screen Recorder. Do not leave it in Downloads - ChromeOS can clean that folder out automatically.
    Mac: Move it to your home folder, e.g. ~/AFS Screen Recorder.
    Windows: Move it to Documents\AFS Screen Recorder.
  3. Open Chrome and go to chrome://extensions.
  4. Turn on Developer mode using the toggle in the top-right corner.
  5. Click Load unpacked (top-left) and select the unzipped folder - the one containing manifest.json. On ChromeOS, you can also drag the folder directly onto the extensions page.
  6. The AFS Screen Recorder tile appears. Click the puzzle-piece icon in the toolbar and pin it so it's always visible.
On a Mac, grant Screen Recording once. macOS requires it before Chrome can capture. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security → Screen Recording, tick Google Chrome, then relaunch Chrome. Without this, recordings come out blank. Windows needs no such step.
Reload your tabs once after installing. The on-page webcam head only appears on pages opened or reloaded after the extension is installed. Open a fresh tab, or reload the page you want to record.

How to record

  1. Click the AFS Screen Recorder icon in the Chrome toolbar.
  2. Pick your Camera and Microphone: toggle each on or off, and choose the device from the dropdowns. If the device names are blank the first time, click Allow camera & mic to list devices. Your choices are remembered.
  3. Click Start Recording.
  4. A large Choose what to record window opens. Chrome asks what to capture - choose a screen, window, or tab, then click Share. The big window shrinks to a small toolbar the moment you choose. Tip: choose a window or tab to keep the little controls toolbar out of the video.
  5. A 3-2-1 countdown plays, then recording begins. Your webcam head appears on the page - drag it wherever you want and that is exactly where it sits in the video.
  6. Pause or stop anytime from the buttons beneath your webcam head (faint until you hover over the head, then solid), or use the slim recorder toolbar or the extension icon (handy if the toolbar gets buried behind other windows). Chrome's native Stop sharing bar also ends it cleanly.
  7. Want to point things out? Hit the Cursor button on the webcam head to switch on a red spotlight that follows your mouse, so viewers can see exactly where you are pointing. Hit it again to turn it off.
  8. A result page opens automatically. Click Download to save the .webm file, or click Open My Drive to go to Google Drive and drag the file in for a shareable link.
First time on a website. Chrome asks for camera permission so the head can show on that page. Click Allow once and it is remembered for that site.

Troubleshooting

The webcam head does not appear on some pages

Chrome does not allow extensions to inject onto chrome:// pages, the Chrome Web Store, or the built-in PDF viewer, so the head cannot show there. Also note the head only appears on pages opened or reloaded after the extension was installed - reload the tab if it is missing. Screen recording itself still works regardless; use the recorder toolbar or the extension icon to control it.

My webcam is not in the recording

When you first click Start Recording, Chrome will prompt for camera permission. Click Allow. If you previously dismissed or blocked the prompt, click the lock or tune icon in the address bar, find Camera, set it to Allow, and reload the page before trying again.

"Your file couldn't be accessed" / ERR_FILE_NOT_FOUND

Chrome reads the extension directly from the folder you selected during install. If that folder was moved, renamed, or deleted (common if it was left in Downloads), Chrome can no longer find the files. To fix: go to chrome://extensions, find AFS Screen Recorder, and click the reload button (circular arrow). If the folder is gone entirely, re-download the zip, unzip it to a permanent location (see install step 2), remove the broken extension, and load it fresh.

The video will not play in some apps

The recording is saved as a .webm file. It plays natively in Chrome and in Google Drive's built-in player. If another app cannot open it, upload it to Google Drive and play it there, or share the Drive link directly.

How it works

Chrome Extension (MV3) MediaRecorder desktopCapture Content Script IndexedDB

1 Architecture

Popup (icon)
device pickers + live pause/stop controls
→opens
Recorder Window
opens the screen picker, records the chosen surface + mic with MediaRecorder
→flags
Content Script
draggable webcam head on the page, filmed in place

Manifest V3, with no background service worker. A slim recorder window does the actual capture (a normal window is the only context allowed to open the screen picker) and records the chosen surface directly. A content script paints the single draggable webcam head onto the page, which the screen capture films in place. The finished video is handed to the result page through IndexedDB, and pause/stop travel between the popup and the recorder window via a small storage flag.

FileRole
manifest.jsonPermissions (desktopCapture, storage) and content script registration
src/popup.html/jsSetup (camera/mic device pickers) and live recording controls on the icon
src/recorder.html/jsSlim toolbar window - opens the screen picker, records screen + mic, saves the video
src/content.js/cssInjects the single draggable webcam head onto the page
src/result.html/jsPost-recording page: inline preview, download button, Drive link
src/lib/Shared utilities: MIME detection, filename generation, IndexedDB recording store

2 Footprint and cost

Effectively zero. The extension records fully locally with no accounts or API keys and nothing to pay for. Network activity is limited to two things: the Google Drive tab that opens when you choose to share (your own Drive), and a single anonymous +1 sent to a tiny usage counter when you save a recording - that is what powers the team tally at the top of this page. No identity, no content, just a number. The counter runs on a small Cloudflare Worker, free tier.

No ongoing cost. Unlike Loom or similar tools, there is no monthly fee, no storage limit, and no third-party data handling.

3 Privacy and security

  • Recordings never leave your machine unless you explicitly upload them.
  • The extension stores nothing remotely and has no backend of any kind.
  • Camera and microphone are captured and recorded entirely in the browser.
  • The one thing the extension sends out is an anonymous +1 to the team usage counter when you save a recording - no account, no identity, no video, just a tally.
  • Sharing is a deliberate manual step - drag the file into Drive yourself.

4 Limitations

  • The webcam head overlays web pages only, not native desktop apps or chrome:// pages. If you record a non-browser app, the head will not appear in that part of the video.
  • Recording the whole screen also films the small controls toolbar. Record a specific tab or window to keep it out of the video.
  • The first time the head appears on a website, Chrome asks for camera permission for that page. Tabs opened before installing the extension need one reload before the head shows.
  • On macOS, Chrome needs Screen Recording permission (System Settings → Privacy & Security → Screen Recording) or recordings come out blank.
  • Output format is .webm. It plays in Chrome and Google Drive. For other apps, upload to Drive and use the Drive player or shareable link.
  • Sharing requires a manual drag into Google Drive. There is no one-click "copy link" built in.
  • Internal AFS use only. Not published to the Chrome Web Store.
